Problem 1: Breakaway and break-in (20 points)

The loop transfer function is

L(s)=KL0​(s),L0​(s)=s(s+2)s+3​,K≥0.

(a) [4 points] Write the characteristic equation in polynomial and Evans forms. Identify the branch starts, finite endpoint, branch count, symmetry, real-axis portions, and asymptote, including its centroid.

(b) [6 points] Form K(s) and use dK/ds=0 to find the real-axis multiple-root candidates. Check each against the real-axis rule and K≥0. Find the gain at each accepted point and identify breakaway versus break-in. Use Rule 5 to show the local incoming and outgoing directions, then complete the hand sketch. A proof of the exact curved-branch shape is not required.

(c) [4 points] Test s=−2+j2​ using both the angle and magnitude conditions. Show the individual vector angles, find the gain, and report all closed-loop poles at that gain.

(d) [2 points] Determine the gain interval for asymptotic stability, including the status of K=0.

(e) [4 points] Submit a MATLAB rlocus plot with the calculated break points and trial-point poles labeled. Use equal real and imaginary axis scales and briefly compare it with your sketch.

Single-asymptote hint, also useful in Problem 4: If L0​=cb(s)/a(s) with c>0, monic a,b, and dega=degb+1, polynomial division gives a/b=s−σa​+O(1/s). Thus the escaping root satisfies s≈σa​−cK at large gain. A positive centroid is an offset in this relation and does not by itself imply an unstable high-gain pole. Preserve any fixed scale factor c when interpreting MATLAB’s parameter.

Problem 3: Geometry of a noncollocated servo (25 points)

Disk-head positioning motivates studying systems whose actuator and sensor are at different locations. Here the supplied model is a rigid platform on compliant mounts, an original numerical adaptation of the course platform model.

Figure 1. Opposite-side actuation and sensing on the platform; small motions are assumed.

Let x be center-of-mass translation and θ be small rotation. Positive coefficients and symmetric support properties decouple translation and rotation. The actuator and sensor offsets are +a and −b, respectively, with a,b>0. The equations and transfer function are supplied:

mx¨+cx​x˙+kx​x=u,Jθ¨+cθ​θ˙+kθ​θ=au,y=x−bθ,

G(s)=(ms2+cx​s+kx​)(Js2+cθ​s+kθ​)(J−abm)s2+(cθ​−abcx​)s+kθ​−abkx​​.

In normalized coordinates, use

m=1,cx​=2,kx​=1,cθ​=2,kθ​=4,β=ab,

so that

G(s;β,J)=(s+1)2(Js2+2s+4)(J−β)s2+(2−2β)s+4−β​.

Fix the proportional controller at Dc​=3. In parts (a)–(d) and (f), hold J=1 and vary the sensor offset, keeping the body and actuator unchanged. Compare β=0.5,1,1.2,1.5; the physical comparison interval is 0.5≤β≤1.5. Noncollocation permits, but does not require, a right-half-plane zero.

(a) [4 points] Find the plant zeros at the four specified values of β. Explain what happens at β=1 and why the plant poles remain fixed during this geometry sweep. Identify the poles and zeros of this auxiliary model and distinguish them from the physical plant’s poles and zeros. The controller remains fixed at 3. For the sign convention, see Root Locus I, §8.

(c) [5 points] The following factorization is supplied, with μ=3β​:

P(s,β)=[s2+(2−μ)s+4−μ][s2+(2+μ)s+4+μ].

Use the quadratics to find the departure directions from the repeated starting poles, sketch the branches, and find the closed-loop stability boundary and crossing frequency. A quartic Routh calculation is not required. For completing the full mathematical sketch, you may use these later features without deriving them: at β=4, the complex branches meet the real axis at s=−1±3​; thereafter two branches approach −1 and the others go to opposite infinities. Concentrate your detailed interpretation on the physical comparison interval.

Use the normalized motor and supplied controller proportions

G(s)=s(s+1)1​,D0​(s)=5s+8+s4​=s5s2+8s+4​,Dc​(s)=λD0​(s),λ>0.

Thus (kP​,kI​,kD​)=λ(8,4,5): one shared multiplier varies all three gains together. Download ps5_servo.m into your MATLAB working folder. It requires Control System Toolbox and supplies the simulation, noise record, and response metrics. You do not need to write noise-generation code.

(a) [7 points] Derive the characteristic polynomial and Evans form 1+λL0,λ​(s)=0. Sketch the gain locus by hand, identifying starts and endpoints, the real-axis portion, the asymptote and centroid, and the departure directions from the double origin. (b) [5 points] Compare λ=0.25,0.5,1 using result = ps5_servo(lambda,Inf). Here Inf explicitly selects the unfiltered controller. Submit the MATLAB gain locus with all poles at these gains marked, a full step-response comparison, and a compact table of overshoot and 1% settling time. Select a gain satisfying at most 10% overshoot and at most 4 s settling. Freeze this gain for all remaining parts.

For the selected unfiltered setting, the supplied measurement noise gives true-output error standard deviation of approximately 0.00222, or 0.222% of the unit setpoint. The next task is to reduce this residual jitter while preserving an acceptable transient response. Add a low-pass filter to the whole controller:

Fp​(s)=s+pp​,Dc,p​(s)=λD0​(s)Fp​(s),p>0.

The physical filter pole is at −p; p is a positive parameter in s−1. Its numerator preserves unity DC gain. This filters the whole PID, whereas another common implementation filters only its derivative term.

(c) [7 points] Define a(s)=s2(s+1) and b(s)=5s2+8s+4. Starting with 1+Dc,p​G=0, collect terms in p and derive Evans form 1+pL0,p​(s)=0 with λ held fixed. Write the resulting characteristic polynomial for your selected gain. Determine the stable range of p and the imaginary-axis crossing. Treat p=0 only as a limiting endpoint, not an operating choice.

(d) [5 points] Sketch this filter-parameter locus by hand. Identify starts with multiplicities, finite endpoints, real-axis portions, the asymptote and centroid, and departure directions from the origin. Explain what the auxiliary endpoints represent physically. For the selected gain, use the supplied breakaway at (s,p)≈(−0.773802,0.111628) and break-in at (−9.063428,18.751541) to connect the branches; no hand solution of a break-point quartic is required. MATLAB plotting and simulation guide

rlocus varies a scalar multiplier on the fixed model supplied to it. For a geometry or filter-pole sweep, pass the derived auxiliary model. Plotting the physical loop with a fixed geometry or filter would vary controller gain instead. The following scaffolding leaves the model and parameter selection to your analysis.

After defining L0, parameterGrid, and selectedValues, you can plot its MATLAB locus on ordinary axes for easy annotation:

R = rlocus(L0,parameterGrid);  % Each column contains all roots at one value.
figure
plot(real(R.'),imag(R.'),'Color',[0.15 0.35 0.65]); hold on
P = pole(L0); Z = zero(L0);
plot(real(P),imag(P),'kx','MarkerSize',9,'LineWidth',1.5)
plot(real(Z),imag(Z),'ko','MarkerSize',9,'LineWidth',1.5)
Q = rlocus(L0,selectedValues);
plot(real(Q(:)),imag(Q(:)),'rs','MarkerSize',7)
xline(0,'k:'); yline(0,'k:'); grid on
xlabel('Real part (s^{-1})'); ylabel('Imaginary part (s^{-1})')

% Use axis equal for Problems 1 and 3.

Choose a parameter grid fine enough to resolve the bends and include the requested values. You may also use MATLAB’s automatic rlocus(L0) plot. For Problem 3, use different markers or colors for the four geometries and highlight the interval from 0.5 to 1.5. For Problem 4’s second filter view, repeat the plot and set xlim([-1.5 0.5]); ylim([-2.5 2.5]).

The helper returns a structure. result.stepInfo.Overshoot is a percentage and result.stepInfo.SettlingTime is in seconds. result.errorStd uses std(result.outputError(result.keep),0). result.poles, result.tailMaxError, and the noise-free response support your transient-removal explanation. Calling ps5_servo(lambda,p) without an output variable also draws diagnostic plots. All runs use the same noise record, a 0.001 s time step, and zero initial states; unstable settings are rejected.

For the filter comparison, first set lambda to your selected value, then use:

comparison = array2table(metrics,'VariableNames', ...
