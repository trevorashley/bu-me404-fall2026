# ODE Background
> *Question:* I haven't taken differential equations in several years. How can I refresh my understanding?

Having a strong understanding of (systems of) linear ordinary differential equations will be foundational to your success in this course. You can assume that anything fromm your prior ODEs course is fair game for this course. _ODEs are a foundational language for control systems - that is, modeling and design/analysis of feedback systems._

That said, here are the references I most recommend as a _refresher_ (i.e., not as a first course):
- S. Brunton, _Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems_, [YouTube Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9fQkLQZe3u8&list=PLMrJAkhIeNNTYaOnVI3QpH7jgULnAmvPA&pp=0gcJCbwFa94AFGB0).
    - Steve is an _excellent_ lecturer, and his presentations are dynamic, colorful, and thorough. He excels at bringing the student to an intuition rather than a complex proof. If you need an audio track to learn better, I _highly_ recommend Steve's series.
- E. Kreyszig, _Advanced Engineering Mathematics_, 10th Edition, Wiley, 2011.
    - This is a standard textbook that covers not just ODEs but many other ubiquitous math topics. The first six chapters in this source will provide you all you need for the course, including Laplace transforms.
- G. Sanderson, "Differential Equations: Solving the Unsolvable", [YouTube Link](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZHQObOWTQDNPOjrT6KVlfJuKtYTftqH6).
    - Grant Sanderson, also known as "3blue1brown", is most known for his incredible visualizations of mathematical concepts. He's also an excellent teacher, and I've personally benefitted from his videos. I highly recommend watching these, perhaps at minimum to add some extra intuition to concepts you may already know.

As you may remember from your preliminary course on ODEs, _linear algebra_ also provides us a boon for solving and analyzing systems of differential equations. Although this course will mostly focus on _classical control_, which revolves around the $s$-domain, most control today is performed in the time-domain using _state space_ methods. As you continue in your control eduation (perhaps through taking ME501), you'll need a refresher on linear algebra. Here are the sources I recommend for that:

- E. Kreyszig, _Advanced Engineering Mathematics_, 10th Edition, Wiley, 2011.
    - Yes, this text is once again relevant. Focus on chapters 7 and 8 for some helpful information.
- G. Strang, [MIT OCW](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/video_galleries/video-lectures/)
    - Gilbert Strang is one of the best lecturers on this topic, so this series is worth a mention. It's not a review - it's truly a series of long lectures. However, if you haven't received the Strang treatment of linear algebra, do yourself a favor and watch these. It'll change your perspective of linear algebra.
- G. Sanderson, "Essence of Linear Algebra", [YouTube Link](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZHQObOWTQDPD3MizzM2xVFitgF8hE_ab)
    - Once again, another excellent series by Grant. Highly recommend watching these.
