### What to submit

Submit only a corrections addendum, with the subparts you reattempt arranged in exam order. Keep your original graded exam; the instructor already has an electronic copy, so you do not need to return or resubmit it.

Put your name on the addendum and label each correction by question and subpart, such as **I.4** or **P2(e)**. For an in-person submission, submit the correction pages together. For an electronic submission, submit the addendum as one _legible_ PDF; please use a PDF scanner app and do not just send pictures.

For each subpart you reattempt:

1. **Identify the issue.** In one or two sentences, explain what was incorrect or missing in your original answer. If you left the subpart blank, say so and identify the principle or method needed to start it.
2. **Show the corrected work.** State the governing equations, assumptions, and intermediate steps needed to justify your answer. Include diagrams, units, stability checks, or explanations when the question calls for them. A replacement final answer alone is insufficient.
3. **Explain the correction.** Briefly explain why your revised reasoning resolves the issue. This may be included alongside your calculations; a separate paragraph is unnecessary.

You do not need to rewrite correct work. If an earlier correction changes a later result, update the affected calculations and conclusions, including those that originally received credit through error carried forward. Reference unchanged work clearly so the revised reasoning can be followed from start to finish.

### How credit is calculated

Corrections are evaluated using the original exam's point allocations and grading criteria. For each subpart, you receive **half of the additional points earned** by your original work together with your corrections. Incomplete or partly correct corrections can earn proportionate credit; participation does **not** lower your original score.

If a subpart originally earned $O$ points and the combined work earns $C$ points under the original rubric, its updated score is

$$
O+\frac{1}{2}\max(0,C-O).
$$

Here, $C$ cannot exceed the subpart's original maximum. Points already awarded, including credit for correctly carrying forward an earlier error, cannot be earned a second time.

| Original score | Score warranted by combined work | Points added | Updated score |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 6/20 | 20/20 | 7 | 13/20 |
| 6/20 | 16/20 | 5 | 11/20 |
| 0/4 (blank) | 4/4 | 2 | 2/4 |

A fully corrected exam originally worth 50/100 becomes 75/100. To earn the maximum available correction credit, supply all missing reasoning and correct all errors in the subparts you reattempt, together with the brief explanations requested above.

If you believe your original work was graded incorrectly, identify that concern separately. An actual grading error is corrected in full before reattempt credit is calculated.
